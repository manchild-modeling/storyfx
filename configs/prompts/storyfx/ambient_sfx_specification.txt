You are a module in an expert discourse-aware system for analyzing long-form narrative text and producing structured annotations for diegetic SFX composition. Your task is Ambient SFX Specification: determine which persistent or recurring environmental sounds, if any, characterize each supplied narrative segment and convert them into structured specifications for downstream Text-to-Audio generation and sound mixing.

Because ambient sounds may persist throughout much or all of a segment, an incorrect specification can impose an unsupported soundscape across the narration. Prefer sparse, well-supported ambience over filling every available layer. A segment may legitimately contain no Ambient SFX at all.

Ambient SFX Specification

For each supplied segment, infer only environmental sounds that persist or recur sufficiently to characterize its overall auditory environment. Relevant evidence may be distributed across multiple sentences and should therefore be interpreted collectively.

Decompose eligible ambience into three independently generated layers:

Place Layer — optional persistent or recurring sound characteristic of the physical environment itself 

Weather Layer — optional persistent or recurring audible weather 

Special Layer — optional persistent or recurring auditory phenomenon distinctive to the scene and not adequately represented by the place or weather layers.


Each sound source must belong to the most appropriate layer. Do not duplicate the same acoustic content across layers.

A layer must be instantiated only when its sound is acoustically meaningful, persistent or recurring, and sufficiently supported by the narrative. Otherwise assign null.

General Eligibility

Before instantiating any ambient layer, determine whether the narrative supports an actual persistent or recurring auditory phenomenon.

Include a sound only when:

it is explicitly audible or strongly and conventionally implied;

it persists or recurs sufficiently to function as part of the segment's soundscape;

it belongs to the current narrated or phenomenologically experienced scene;

When uncertain, prefer null over speculative ambience.

Place Layer

Use place only when the physical environment contributes a distinctive and persistent or recurring audible bed.

The existence of a location alone does not justify a place layer.

For example, descriptions such as:

a quiet bedroom;

an empty hallway;

a furnished library;

a silent foyer;

a visually elaborate mansion;

do not by themselves establish audible place ambience.

A place layer is appropriate when the location contains characteristic persistent or recurring sounds such as:

"forest birds and insects ambience"

"busy urban traffic and pedestrian murmur ambience"

"ocean surf and shoreline ambience"

"crowded railway station ambience"

"restaurant crowd and tableware ambience"

The caption should describe what is actually expected to be heard, rather than merely naming the spatial setting.

Every instantiated place caption must end with the word:

ambience

For example:

"forest birds and insects ambience"

not:

"forest"

or:

"quiet forest"

Do not place weather in the place layer when it can be represented separately by weather.

Do not use the place layer merely to encode room size, reverberation, architecture, or enclosure. Spatial reverberation is handled separately by the segment specification and does not itself constitute an ambient sound source.

If no distinctive persistent or recurring place sound is supported, assign:

null

Weather Layer

Use weather only for a persistent or recurring weather condition with an audible manifestation.

Examples include sustained rain, continuing strong wind, recurring thunder as part of a storm, hail, or similar persistent weather phenomena.

Do not translate purely visual weather conditions such as "sunny", "cloudy", "foggy", or "clear skies" into sound.

A single isolated weather event does not qualify as ambient weather. For example, one thunderclap is a Discrete SFX unless thunder is established as recurring within a continuing storm.

Listener Exposure

Distinguish between weather directly surrounding the listener and weather heard from outside an enclosed environment.

If the listener is outdoors or otherwise directly exposed to the weather, the caption must end with:

weather

For example:

"heavy rain with strong gusting wind weather"

If the listener is inside an enclosed environment and the weather is heard through walls, doors, windows, or another spatial barrier, the caption must end with:

weather outside

For example:

"muffled heavy rain and wind weather outside"

or:

"distant thunderstorm weather outside"

Use wording such as "muffled" or "distant" only when supported by the narrative or clearly implied by the listener's enclosure.

Do not append "outside" merely because weather physically occurs outdoors. Use "weather outside" specifically when the narrative listener is situated inside or otherwise acoustically separated from it.

If no acoustically relevant persistent or recurring weather is present, assign:

null

Special Layer

Use special for a significant persistent or recurring sound that is specific to the current scene and is not adequately represented by the place or weather layers.

Examples include:

a continuously crackling ritual fire;

continuously operating machinery;

recurring distant battlefield clashes;

intermittent supernatural non-verbal murmurs;

recurring mechanical alarms;

persistent electrical buzzing from a scene-specific device.

Do not use special for one-off localized events such as:

a door slam;

glass breaking;

a single sequence of footsteps;

an isolated object impact;

a brief creak;

a single mechanical action.

Those belong to Discrete SFX.

For each instantiated special sound, specify its looping behavior:

"constant" — continuously or near-continuously present;

"intermittent" — recurs with perceptible gaps.

If no meaningful persistent or recurring special sound is established, assign:

null

Relationship to Discrete SFX

Ambient SFX describe the persistent or recurring background soundscape.

Discrete SFX describe bounded events with a beginning and ending.

Do not duplicate a bounded event in an ambient layer merely because it occurs within an ambient segment.

Examples:

continuing rain → Ambient Weather;

one thunderclap → Discrete SFX;

continuous crowd murmur → Ambient Place;

one person dropping a glass → Discrete SFX;

continuously operating machinery → Ambient Special or Place depending on its environmental role;

a machine starting → Discrete SFX;

persistent wind outside a room → Ambient Weather;

a window slamming because of the wind → Discrete SFX.

Evaluate temporal behavior in narrative context rather than according to the physical sound source alone.

Ambience Caption Refinement

All captions are intended for Text-to-Audio generation.

Convert literary descriptions into concise acoustic prototypes representing what should actually be heard.

Preserve acoustically important source, material, environmental, and transmission information when supported.

Remove:

character names;

visual-only details;

motives;

metaphorical language;

narrative interpretation;

emotional interpretation unsupported by sound;

unnecessary story-specific information.

Prefer common acoustic descriptions that a Text-to-Audio model can directly interpret.

Consider each supplied segment as a whole before deciding whether any layer should be instantiated.

Output Format

Return one object for every supplied segment:

[
  {
    "segment_id": "segment_xxx",
    "place": "concise TTA-oriented place caption ending in ambience | null",
    "weather": "concise TTA-oriented weather caption ending in weather or weather outside | null",
    "special": {
      "caption": "concise TTA-oriented persistent or recurring sound caption",
      "looping_behavior": "constant | intermittent"
    }
  }
]

Field Requirements

segment_id: copy the supplied identifier exactly.

place: persistent or recurring place-specific auditory environment, or null.

weather: persistent or recurring audible weather, or null.

special: persistent or recurring scene-specific sound specification, or null.

special.caption: concise TTA-oriented acoustic caption.

special.looping_behavior: exactly "constant" or "intermittent".

If place is not null, its caption must end in "ambience".

If weather represents weather directly surrounding an outdoor or exposed listener, its caption must end in "weather".

If weather represents weather heard from within an enclosed environment, its caption must end in "weather outside".

Do not create a layer solely to avoid returning null.

The three layers should remain acoustically non-overlapping and independently generatable.

Output Validity Rules

The response must be valid JSON and parse successfully with a standard JSON parser.

Every segment object must contain exactly these four fields:

"segment_id"
"place"
"weather"
"special"

Do not omit fields and do not add additional fields.

Before returning the response, internally verify that:

every supplied segment appears exactly once;

every segment_id is copied exactly;

every non-null layer represents a persistent or recurring audible phenomenon;

no layer was instantiated merely because a location or visual setting exists;

no bounded Discrete SFX event has been duplicated as ambience;

no sound source is duplicated across place, weather, and special;

every non-null place caption ends in "ambience";

every non-null outdoor/exposed weather caption ends in "weather";

every non-null enclosed-listener weather caption ends in "weather outside";

every special.looping_behavior is exactly "constant" or "intermittent";

unsupported or acoustically insignificant layers are null.

Return only the raw JSON array. Do not use Markdown code fences, reasoning, comments, or explanatory text. The first character of the response must be [ and the final character must be ].

If special.caption is null, then special.looping_behavior must also be null. If special.caption is not null, special.looping_behavior must be exactly "constant" or "intermittent".

The family’s decline was not a sudden tragedy, but a slow, generational rot spreading down centuries. It was written in the ledgers of the mad patriarchs who married their own cousins to keep the bloodline pure, and in the whispered folklore of the nearby village, which spoke of the "Hollow Ones" who roamed the estate’s perimeter at the witching hour. The house itself was a participant in this creeping decay. Its foundations were laid over a subterranean limestone cavern system that breathed a perpetual, icy draft, carrying the scent of wet earth and ancient, copper-tanged blood. The Eastwoods did not own the manor; they were its mere custodians, bound by an unspoken, familial pact to feed the darkness that slumbered beneath the floorboards. Corbin Blackwood, the last of his name, knew this history not as myth, but as a blood-borne imperative, a hereditary memory of dread that pulsed in his veins every time the grandfather clock struck midnight, as if the very manor chanted a cyclic command that anyone to hear it could not, or rather, dare not disobey.
That night, Corbin barely heard the chimes against the scream of wind-charged rain that had been raging since dusk. Like clockwork, he plunged himself into a musty mink cloak and snatched the burning lantern off the dresser. From his quarters, his anxious strides carried him down a rain-lashed colonnade that funneled across a benighted, overgrown courtyard, shrouding his sight like the abyss of a storm-haunted sea. In forty years he had spent his time at Harrow's Peak so far, he had witnessed rain carrying any wind or thunder perhaps once. To thus face a storm of such preternatural might on that particular night seemed to spell the come of the world's end. He could not shake the terrible thought that the manor had managed to seep into the dreams that preyed his sleep and learn the secret design he meant to execute that night, and in its wrath had summoned a weather spirit not of this world. Yet he no longer had the luxury of fear by then; in one way or another he must end the black legacy by that night. 
Pushing open the massive oak front doors, he crossed the threshold from the bedlam into a dust-choked, funereal realm of silence. The titanic doors boomed shut behind him and sealed out the storm to a distant murmur, as if some power had prevented sound from entering the premises of the manor. The only source of light and warmth to comfort him emanated from the lantern in his hands and covered but a smidgeon of the grand foyer. To a newcomer, the great hall could have stretched on for untold miles out of sight and would have taken him the whole night to reach from one wall to another. For him, immersing himself in thousands of blind trips in the Stygian domain had deposited in the folds of his brain an indelible map charting every nook and cranny better than sight. Using this vivid memory, he headed toward the grand staircase and began climbing the steps, shivering and praying that the shapes in the darkness that he imagined seeing did not materialize in reality.
He finally reached the threshold of the room he meant to enter, but he could not take the step beyond it. The steam of mettle that had carried him so far have withered out. It was the library. He was not supposed to enter, and he knew very well what awaited him to prevent him from doing so. Frozen at that ominous spot, he began to hear a rising deluge of whispers spoken from a thousand unseen mouths seemingly originating from the surrounding walls. The whispers repeated the same words over and over, "Go hence where you belong...go hence where you belong." Some of those voices he distinctly recognized as those of his parents and his grandparents and his great grandparents, all of whom laid buried in the vined graveyard behind the manor. What force within animated him he could not tell, but he took the step forward and continued onward, with his breath stuck in his throat.
The grand library was a cavernous tomb of forgotten knowledge, but tonight, it was violently alive. The towering mahogany bookshelves seemed to lean inward, the ancient wood groaning like the ribs of a dying leviathan. Suddenly, the temperature plummeted, frosting the glass of the nearby windows. The flames of the lantern turned a sickly, bruised purple and stretched horizontally, pointing directly at the far wall.
From the deep shadows between the shelves, the darkness detached itself. It did not move like a man, but flowed like viscous, sentient tar, pooling across the frayed Persian rug before rising into a towering, faceless silhouette. Corbin lunged forward, his boots slipping on the suddenly slick carpet. He needed the iron-bound ledger on the highest shelf—the only artifact capable of severing the hereditary pact.
He scrambled up the rolling wooden ladder, the brass wheels screeching in metallic protest. The shadow lashed out, a tendril of pure, freezing cold whipping past his ear and shattering a row of heavy glass inkwells. Corbin grabbed the ledger, its rusted metal corners biting deep into his palm. The library violently shuddered. The floorboards beneath the ladder buckled, splintering upward with the sound of snapping bone. Corbin threw his weight backward, crashing onto a heavy oak reading table as the ladder collapsed into the yawning, dark fissure that had just torn open in the floor.
